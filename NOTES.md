# NOTES — Five Seconds to Explain the Glass

Task: one sentence, at most 200 characters, in a house cat's own voice:
the excuse it gives in the five seconds after sweeping a glass off the
table. No apology and no confession — the most convincing case a cat can
make for itself. Judge (pairwise): it must be a single-sentence cat
excuse about the glass; then cat logic with no remorse, a fresh comic
idea (not the obvious gravity / slippery-edge / it-was-already-falling
lines), fitting five seconds of spoken delivery, and no padding.

## Variants

### vA — 125 chars — PICKED

> It was sitting in my sunbeam, judging me, so I relocated it to the floor — a public service, really, and I would do it again.

Cat logic throughout: the glass is the aggressor (sitting in MY sunbeam,
judging ME), the knock is a relocation and a public service, and the
ending — "I would do it again" — is total lack of remorse in five words.
Fresh: no gravity, no slip, no apology.

### vB — 133 chars

> I was conducting a vital gravity experiment and the glass volunteered, so technically this is science and you should be taking notes.

Funny, but the judge brief explicitly docks the obvious gravity line —
this variant walks straight into it.

### vC — 119 chars

> The glass looked at me wrong, occupied my favourite spot, and chose violence first, so I was simply the paw of justice.

Cat logic and no remorse, but "chose violence" is a borrowed meme phrase
and "paw of justice" is a weaker ending than vA's "I would do it again".

## Why vA

Against the judge criteria: (1) one sentence, a cat excusing knocking a
glass off the table; (2) pure cat self-interest, no apology anywhere;
(3) a fresh idea — the glass as a sunbeam-squatting judge, the knock as a
public service; (4) a spoken line deliverable in five seconds; (5) 125/200,
every word earning its place.
